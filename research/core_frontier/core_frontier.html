Core Frontier Investment Portfolio

65% core investments (crypto, hard assets, energy and space, with SpaceX as the core growth asset) and 35% short-term trading sleeves: a VIX spike fade, a market-neutral crypto funding-carry sleeve, momentum long/short and prediction-market models (Forward Factor calendars are tracked as a paper pilot). The trading sleeves are chosen to make money when the core is losing it. A margin-capped leverage layer sits on top.

Backtest October 2015 – 6 November 2026, monthly, net of trading costs and borrowing; data refreshed to the 6 November 2026 close. 2021–2026 is the untouched test period. Trading sleeves (35%)

VIX spike fade · 15%

  • Rule: short VIX futures (0.5× the sleeve) only after a spike has peaked. That means VIX ≥ 25, the term structure back in contango, and VIX 15% below its 10-day high. Exit below VIX 18, after 40 days, or the moment VIX ≥ VIX3M again.
  • Why it fits: it sells volatility when premiums are richest, after the crash rather than during it. Over 2021–26 it earned 12% a year (Sharpe 0.67). In the earlier VIX study (study_vix.py), the same rule on the real SVXY fund (2011+) matched the model; that check was not re-run here.
  • Guardrails from the Principled Trading Group deck: trade only inside the VIX 16–30 band, stand aside if VVIX tops 120, and keep VIX structures to 2–5% risk each. A VIX-doubling day costs about 7.5% of the whole portfolio at this size.

Momentum long/short · 5%

  • Rule: long the top 10% of US stocks by 12-month momentum and short the bottom 10%, scaled to 12% volatility (survivorship-free CRSP data).
  • Why it fits: it is market-neutral and uncorrelated with the core and the VIX sleeve. It earned 7% a year in 2021–26.

Prediction models · 5% (pilot)

This tests the deck's Keltner-channel dip-buy on BTC itself. It assumes the market prices "Up" at fair random-walk odds. Dips that reach the Keltner band finished above the open more often than those odds imply, while ordinary dips did not. The 1-hour trend filter cut the sample without adding edge. Paper-trade against real Polymarket prices before funding this sleeve.

Funding carry · 10%

  • Rule: buy spot BTC and ETH (50/50) and short the same amount of the Deribit perpetual, so price moves cancel and the position collects the funding that perp longs pay. Each coin is on only while its trailing 7-day funding beats T-bills by 2%/yr, judged on the previous day's data; otherwise that half sits in T-bills. Each switch costs 0.2%.
  • Result: 6.7% a year since May 2019 (5.7% in 2021–26) with a worst drawdown of −1.0%. It was on 39% of the time, and its correlation with the core is 0.15. It has shrunk: in 2025–26, as funding fell, it made 2.4% and 1.3% (to date) against T-bills of 4.3% and 3.2%.
  • Risk: the price risk cancels; the exchange risk doesn't. Split the sleeve across at least two venues and cap each one. Spot crypto is fully funded, so the sleeve adds up to 10% to margin use while it is on (counted in the leverage layer).

Capital-efficiency layer: portfolio margin and leverage

The portfolio-margin deck's idea: risk-based haircuts plus a "triple hedge" (calendars, VIX call backspreads, SPY put ratio spreads) free buying power, and VIX-band leverage (2.0× when VIX < 15, falling to 0.75× above 50) puts it to work. Your goal for this layer was the highest CAGR per unit of drawdown, with no calendar year worse than −15%. We tested every combination on 2015–2020, then checked the picks on 2021–2026.

Leverage is set each morning so that margin use on the positions about to be held stays at or below 70%, the guardrail in the PTG and margin decks. It averaged 1.22× against a 1.75× target, and the cap binds most when crypto positions or the carry sleeve are large.

Calendar-year returns: recommended layer vs no extra leverage

What the layer can and can't do.
  • Whole-portfolio leverage did not improve the return per unit of drawdown out of sample. The unlevered 65/35 had a 2021–26 Calmar of 0.96, against 0.81 with the margin-capped 1.75× layer. The core is already levered inside by its 40% volatility target, so leverage on top mostly adds drawdown.
  • New with the carry sleeve: levering only the trading sleeves did improve it, to 1.03 at 3× (VIX fade capped) (2021–26 CAGR 23.4%). That test does not apply the 70% margin cap, and 3× carry alone would tie up 30% of the account in fully funded spot crypto. It is not adopted until it is re-tested with the cap.
  • The deck's VIX bands did worse than constant leverage. They cut exposure after spikes, just before the rebounds. The loss-budget throttle made the same mistake.
  • The hedges cost more than they saved. The SPY put backspread paid 12% of notional in the COVID crash but cost −0.5% a year overall. The VIX call backspread returned −12% a year per unit.
  • Margin is the binding limit, mostly because of crypto. Spot crypto has to be fully funded, so even the unlevered 65/35 crosses 70% margin use on 22% of days. Holding crypto through CME futures frees margin, but their usual premium over spot and the extra crypto exposure lowered the risk-adjusted result.
The usable form of the layer is a margin-capped leverage target: aim for 1.75× and let the 70% margin cap cut it automatically. In 2021–26 that made 23.8% a year against 22.1%, with a −29% worst drawdown (daily) and no year below −8% in the whole sample, inside your −15% rule.

5 · FF on single stocks and ETFs (Kadan factor model): not validated

Caution: today's real option chains show this modelled back month does not match the real one (table above), so treat this part as a hypothesis. CBOE publishes only a 30-day implied vol for single names: VXAPL, VXAZN, VXGS and VXIBM, plus VXN, RVX, VXEEM, VXEWZ, VXFXI, VXSLV and VXTLT for ETFs. The paper splits each name's implied variance into β² × market implied variance (from VIX/VIX3M) plus an idiosyncratic part. We let the idiosyncratic part mean-revert to its 1-year average, at a speed fitted on 2011–20, to build each name's 93-day vol and from it the FF.

What changes in the plan.
  • Keep core BTC/ETH in spot (or spot ETFs), not perps. Funding is pro-cyclical: it is highest exactly when the trend signal says hold crypto. Perps cost the crypto sleeves 5.7% (BTC) and 5.9% (ETH) a year in 2021–26. The freed margin buys more CAGR (up to 29% vs 23.8%), but the worst drawdown deepens to −49% and the CAGR-to-drawdown ratio falls. Low collateral would also have been liquidated on 12 March 2020.
  • Use perps for carry instead: now in the portfolio at 10%. The market-neutral funding-carry sleeve made 5.7% a year in 2021–26 with a −1.0% worst drawdown and correlation 0.15 with the core. Against the FF calendars it replaced in the 10% slot, the 1.75× layer earns 23.8% vs 23.2% a year in 2021–26 (worst drawdown −29% vs −29%). Spread it across venues and cap any one exchange, because the risk here is the exchange, not the price.
  • FF calendars: the deck works as a crisis trade, and only with good fills. Run as written on SPY/QQQ/IWM/DIA, it reproduced March 2020 (+53% with realistic costs) but adds only +1.2% a year across 2011–26, all of it from a few crisis weeks. The modelled single-stock FF did not validate against real chains. Keep FF as a paper pilot (0% weight, tracked), use the FF < 0.15 exit if it is promoted (holding to expiry turned 2020 negative), and trade only liquid index ETFs with limit orders on the spread. Before sizing it up, re-test on real historical option quotes (e.g. a paid ORATS or OptionMetrics history).

Operating rules

  • Rebalance to 65/35 monthly, or when either side drifts more than 5 points.
  • Margin: keep portfolio-margin use under 70% and de-lever at 80%. Keep at least 20% of each trading sleeve in cash (from the PTG and FF decks).
  • Per-trade risk:
    • VIX structures: 2–5%;
    • FF calendars (paper pilot): 4–10% by VIX band if promoted, half that at first;
    • carry: at most half the sleeve on any one exchange;
    • prediction trades: 0.5–1%, at most 5 open, −3% daily loss limit, 24-hour halt after a 5% sleeve drawdown.
  • Stress budget:
    • a March-2020 style crypto crash day costs about 13.5% of the portfolio at the capped sizes;
    • a VIX-doubling day costs about 7.5%;
    • an exchange failing while the carry sleeve is fully on costs up to 10% (5% if split across two venues).
    Never lift the crypto or VIX caps.
  • Promote a pilot sleeve (FF calendars, prediction models) to full size only after 3 months of live or paper results that match the backtest.
