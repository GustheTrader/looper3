Core Frontier Investment Portfolio

65% core investments (crypto, hard assets, energy and space, with SpaceX as the core growth asset) and 35% short-term trading sleeves: VIX strategies, Forward Factor calendars, momentum long/short and prediction-market models. The trading sleeves are chosen to make money when the core is losing it. A margin-capped leverage layer sits on top.

Backtest October 2015 – November 2026, monthly, net of trading costs and borrowing. 2021–2026 is the untouched test period. Not investment advice.

Trading sleeves (35%)

VIX spike fade · 15%

  • Rule: short VIX futures (0.5× the sleeve) only after a spike has peaked. That means VIX ≥ 25, the term structure back in contango, and VIX 15% below its 10-day high. Exit below VIX 18, after 40 days, or the moment VIX ≥ VIX3M again.
  • Why it fits: it sells volatility when premiums are richest, after the crash rather than during it. Over 2021–26 it earned about 12% a year (Sharpe 0.67). The same rule on the real SVXY fund (2011+) matched the model.
  • Guardrails from the Principled Trading Group deck: trade only inside the VIX 16–30 band, stand aside if VVIX tops 120, and keep VIX structures to 2–5% risk each. A VIX-doubling day costs about 7.5% of the whole portfolio at this size.

Momentum long/short · 5%

  • Rule: long the top 10% of US stocks by 12-month momentum and short the bottom 10%, scaled to 12% volatility (survivorship-free CRSP data).
  • Why it fits: it is market-neutral and uncorrelated with the core and the VIX sleeve. It earned about 7% a year in 2021–26.

Capital-efficiency layer: portfolio margin and leverage

The portfolio-margin deck's idea: risk-based haircuts plus a "triple hedge" (calendars, VIX call backspreads, SPY put ratio spreads) free buying power, and VIX-band leverage (2.0× when VIX < 15, falling to 0.75× above 50) puts it to work. Your goal for this layer was the highest CAGR per unit of drawdown, with no calendar year worse than −15%. We tested every combination on 2015–2020, then checked the picks on 2021–2026.

Calendar-year returns: recommended layer vs no extra leverage

What the layer can and can't do.
  • No leverage setting improved the return per unit of drawdown out of sample. The unlevered 65/35 had the best 2021–26 Calmar (0.94). The core is already levered inside by its 40% volatility target, so leverage on top mostly adds drawdown.
  • The deck's VIX bands did worse than constant leverage. They cut exposure after spikes, just before the rebounds. The loss-budget throttle made the same mistake.
  • The hedges cost more than they saved. The SPY put backspread paid about 12% of notional in the COVID crash but cost about 0.5% a year overall. The VIX call backspread lost about 12% a year per unit.
  • Margin is the binding limit, mostly because of crypto. Spot crypto has to be fully funded, so even the unlevered 65/35 crosses 70% margin use on about a quarter of days. Holding crypto through CME futures frees margin, but their usual premium over spot and the extra crypto exposure lowered the risk-adjusted result.
The usable form of the layer is a margin-capped leverage target: aim for 1.75× and let the 70% margin cap cut it automatically. In 2021–26 that made 24.0% a year against 21.5%, with a −28% worst drawdown and no year below −8% in the whole sample, comfortably inside your −15% rule.

5 · FF on single stocks and ETFs (Kadan factor model): not validated

Caution: today's real option chains show this modelled back month does not match the real one (table above), so treat this part as a hypothesis. CBOE publishes only a 30-day implied vol for single names: VXAPL, VXAZN, VXGS and VXIBM, plus VXN, RVX, VXEEM, VXEWZ, VXFXI, VXSLV and VXTLT for ETFs. The paper splits each name's implied variance into β² × market implied variance (from VIX/VIX3M) plus an idiosyncratic part. We let the idiosyncratic part mean-revert to its 1-year average, at a speed fitted on 2011–20, to build each name's 93-day vol and from it the FF.

Average calendar trade by FF quartile (all weekly entries, before costs)

For stocks and ETFs, a higher FF means better calendars, as the deck claims. On the S&P 500 it is the opposite: high FF there means a crisis spike that keeps going.

The paper's prediction holds for stocks and ETFs: trades entered when implied variance is far above realised do worst, because jumps follow. On the index it is not clear-cut.

What changes in the plan.
  • Keep core BTC/ETH in spot (or spot ETFs), not perps. Funding is pro-cyclical: it is highest exactly when the trend signal says hold crypto. Perps cost the crypto sleeves about 6 points a year in 2021–26. The freed margin buys more CAGR (up to 28% vs 24%), but drawdowns grow from −28% to −38–46% and the CAGR-to-drawdown ratio falls. Low collateral would also have been liquidated on 12 March 2020.
  • Use perps for carry instead. The market-neutral funding-carry sleeve made 5.8%/yr in 2021–26 with a −1% worst drawdown and almost no link to the core (correlation 0.14). It replaces the 10% FF sleeve: the 1.75× layer goes from 23.9% to 24.6% a year with the same drawdown. Spread it across venues and cap any one exchange, because the risk here is the exchange, not the price.
  • FF calendars: the deck works as a crisis trade, and only with good fills. Run as written on SPY/QQQ/IWM/DIA, it reproduced March 2020 (+50% with realistic costs) but adds only about +0.9% a year across 2011–26, all of it from a few crisis weeks. The modelled single-stock FF did not validate against real chains. Keep FF at pilot size, use the FF < 0.15 exit (holding to expiry turned 2020 negative), and trade only liquid index ETFs with limit orders on the spread. Before sizing it up, re-test on real historical option quotes (e.g. a paid ORATS or OptionMetrics history).

Operating rules

  • Rebalance to 65/35 monthly, or when either side drifts more than 5 points.
  • Margin: keep portfolio-margin use under 70% and de-lever at 80%. Keep at least 20% of each trading sleeve in cash (from the PTG and FF decks).
  • Per-trade risk:
    • VIX structures: 2–5%;
    • FF calendars: 4–10% by VIX band (pilot: use half);
    • prediction trades: 0.5–1%, at most 5 open, −3% daily loss limit, 24-hour halt after a 5% sleeve drawdown.
  • Stress budget:
    • a March-2020 style crypto crash day costs about 13.5% of the portfolio at the capped sizes;
    • a VIX-doubling day costs about 7.5%.
    Never lift the crypto or VIX caps.
  • Promote a pilot sleeve (FF calendars, prediction models) to full size only after 3 months of live or paper results that match the backtest.
