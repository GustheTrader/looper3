Core Frontier Investment Portfolio

65% core investments (crypto, hard assets, energy and space, with SpaceX as the core growth asset) and 35% short-term trading sleeves: a VIX spike fade, Forward Factor calendars worked with master execution, a market-neutral crypto funding-carry sleeve and momentum long/short. A prediction-market model and a structural-decay sleeve are tracked on paper only. The trading sleeves are chosen to make money when the core is losing it. A margin-capped leverage layer sits on top.

Backtest October 2015 – [[last_date]], monthly, net of trading costs and borrowing; data refreshed to the [[last_date]] close. 2021–2026 is the untouched test period. Every figure on this page is filled from the result files and re-checked by an independent audit script ([[audit_n]] checks: [[audit_pass]] pass, [[audit_note]] disclosed limitations, [[audit_fail]] failed; table near the end). Trading sleeves (35%)

VIX spike fade · 15%

  • Rule: short VIX futures (0.5× the sleeve) only after a spike has peaked. That means VIX ≥ 25, the term structure back in contango, and VIX 15% below its 10-day high. Exit below VIX 18, after 40 days, or the moment VIX ≥ VIX3M again.
  • Why it fits: it sells volatility when premiums are richest, after the crash rather than during it. Over 2021–26 it earned [[fade_test_cagr]] a year (Sharpe [[fade_test_sharpe]]). In the earlier VIX study (study_vix.py), the same rule on the real SVXY fund (2011+) matched the model; that check was not re-run here.
  • Guardrails from the Principled Trading Group deck: trade only inside the VIX 16–30 band, stand aside if VVIX tops 120, and keep VIX structures to 2–5% risk each. A VIX-doubling day costs about [[vix_double]] of the whole portfolio at this size.

Momentum long/short · 5%

  • Rule: long the top 10% of US stocks by 12-month momentum and short the bottom 10%, scaled to 12% volatility (survivorship-free CRSP data).
  • Why it fits: it is market-neutral and uncorrelated with the core and the VIX sleeve. It earned [[mom_test_cagr]] a year in 2021–26.

Funding carry · [[w_carry]]

  • Rule: buy spot BTC and ETH (50/50) and short the same amount of the Deribit perpetual, so price moves cancel and the position collects the funding that perp longs pay. Each coin is on only while its trailing 7-day funding beats T-bills by 2%/yr, judged on the previous day's data; otherwise that half sits in T-bills. Each switch costs 0.2%.
  • Result: [[carry_full_cagr]] a year since May 2019 ([[carry_test_cagr]] in 2021–26) with a worst drawdown of [[carry_test_dd]].   • Risk: the price risk cancels; the exchange risk doesn't. Split the sleeve across at least two venues and cap each one. Spot crypto is fully funded, so the sleeve adds up to [[w_carry]] to margin use while it is on (counted in the leverage layer).

How to work every option order

  1. One combo order for the whole spread. Never leg in.
  2. Start at mid. Every 5 minutes, improve by 10% of the bid/ask width. Never pay more than mid + 30% of the width (the "Never pay above" column).
  3. Trade 10:00–15:30 New York time. Spreads are widest at the open and into the closing auction.
  4. No fill by 15:30: cancel. Re-quote tomorrow only if the signal still holds.
  5. Once filled, place the exit as a good-till-cancelled limit and keep the kill rules on screen: FF < 0.15, or 7 days before the front expiry.
  6. Size from the VIX band (4/6/8/10% of the FF sleeve) and keep 20% of the sleeve in cash.

Capital-efficiency layer: portfolio margin and leverage

The portfolio-margin deck's idea: risk-based haircuts plus a "triple hedge" (calendars, VIX call backspreads, SPY put ratio spreads) free buying power, and VIX-band leverage (2.0× when VIX < 15, falling to 0.75× above 50) puts it to work. Your goal for this layer was the highest CAGR per unit of drawdown, with no calendar year worse than −15%. We tested every combination on 2015–2020, then checked the picks on 2021–2026.

What the layer can and can't do.
  • Whole-portfolio leverage did not improve the return per unit of drawdown out of sample. The unlevered 65/35 had a 2021–26 Calmar of [[unlev_calmar]], against [[rec_calmar]] with the margin-capped [[L_rec]] layer. The core is already levered inside by its 40% volatility target, so leverage on top mostly adds drawdown.
  • New with the carry sleeve: levering only the trading sleeves did improve it, to [[cl_best_calmar]] at [[cl_best_k]] (2021–26 CAGR [[cl_best_cagr]]). That test does not apply the 70% margin cap, and 3× carry alone would tie up 30% of the account in fully funded spot crypto. It is not adopted until it is re-tested with the cap.
  • The deck's VIX bands did worse than constant leverage. They cut exposure after spikes, just before the rebounds. The loss-budget throttle made the same mistake.
  • The hedges cost more than they saved. The SPY put backspread paid [[hedge_spy_covid]] of notional in the COVID crash but cost [[hedge_spy_yr]] a year overall. The VIX call backspread returned [[hedge_vix_yr]] a year per unit.
  • Margin is the binding limit, mostly because of crypto. Spot crypto has to be fully funded, so even the unlevered 65/35 crosses 70% margin use on [[unlev_over70]] of days. Holding crypto through CME futures frees margin, but their usual premium over spot and the extra crypto exposure lowered the risk-adjusted result.
The usable form of the layer is a margin-capped leverage target: aim for [[L_rec]] (the layer's own 2015–20 pick) and let the 70% margin cap cut it automatically. In 2021–26 that made [[rec_test_cagr]] a year against [[unlev_test_cagr]], with a [[rec_test_dd]] worst drawdown (daily) and no year below [[rec_worst_year]] in the whole sample, inside your −15% rule.

5 · FF on single stocks and ETFs (Kadan factor model): not validated

Caution: today's real option chains show this modelled back month does not match the real one (table above), so treat this part as a hypothesis. CBOE publishes only a 30-day implied vol for single names: VXAPL, VXAZN, VXGS and VXIBM, plus VXN, RVX, VXEEM, VXEWZ, VXFXI, VXSLV and VXTLT for ETFs. The paper splits each name's implied variance into β² × market implied variance (from VIX/VIX3M) plus an idiosyncratic part. We let the idiosyncratic part mean-revert to its 1-year average, at a speed fitted on 2011–20, to build each name's 93-day vol and from it the FF.

Structural decay (PTG deck): tested, not funded

The deck's idea is real: leveraged and futures-based ETPs lose value mechanically (variance drag, daily rebalancing, roll). Since 2015 (or launch), buy-and-hold lost on every bear and volatility product below. The deck harvests it with 6–9-month debit put spreads (long at-the-money, short 10-delta) when Close < EMA20 < EMA50, ADX(10) > 20 and RSI(2) > 70 (plus contango for VIX products). Exits are the kill switch, a curve inversion, 50% of max profit, or 30 days before expiry. Risk is 1% of the portfolio per position. Option prices are Black-Scholes with each ETP's own realised vol, scaled and skewed to match today's real chains. Bid/ask is what those chains quote.

Why it is not in the portfolio.
  • The edge exists only at mid prices. With no trading costs the deck's trade makes [[dc_A0_d]] a year in 2011–20 and [[dc_A0_t]] in 2021–26. Paying the quoted bid/ask, it makes [[dc_A_d]] and [[dc_A_t]]. Six-to-nine-month options on these products are thin: at-the-money half-spreads of 7–30%, open interest often under 50 contracts.
  • A master protocol helps in-sample but fails out of sample. 90-day tenors (the real holding period is about 50 days), only names with tight markets ([[decay_liquid]]), and one combo order at 30% of the spread give [[dc_C_d]] a year in 2011–20 but [[dc_C_t]] in 2021–26, including [[dc_C_2022]] in 2022.
  • It is a hidden long-equity bet. A put spread on a 3× bear ETF profits when the index rises. In 2022 the index fell, the bear ETFs rallied, and the sleeve lost with the core instead of hedging it.
  • The deck's VIX call backspread is the same structure tested in the margin layer (it lost about 12% a year per unit). The IBIT/BITX pair has under three years of data: BITX trailed 2× bitcoin by about 29 points a year, so the decay is real, but there is too little history to test the trade.

Operating rules

  • Rebalance to 65/35 monthly, or when either side drifts more than 5 points.
  • Margin: keep portfolio-margin use under 70% and de-lever at 80%. Keep at least 20% of each trading sleeve in cash (from the PTG and FF decks).
  • Per-trade risk:
    • VIX structures: 2–5%;
    • FF calendars: 4–10% of the FF sleeve per calendar by VIX band, one combo order each, never above mid + 30% of the spread;
    • carry: at most half the sleeve on any one exchange;
    • prediction trades: 0.5–1%, at most 5 open, −3% daily loss limit, 24-hour halt after a 5% sleeve drawdown.
  • Stress budget:
    • a March-2020 style crypto crash day costs about [[crypto_crash]] of the portfolio at the capped sizes;
    • a VIX-doubling day costs about [[vix_double]];
    • an exchange failing while the carry sleeve is fully on costs up to 10% (5% if split across two venues).
    Never lift the crypto or VIX caps.
  • Promote a paper sleeve (prediction models, structural decay) to full size only after 3 months of live or paper results that match the backtest.
