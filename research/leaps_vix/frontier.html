Frontier Plan: crypto, hard assets, energy and space

The Goal-25 framework rebuilt around Bitcoin, gold and silver, miners, metals, uranium, energy, aerospace and space, with SpaceX as the core growth asset. Seven sleeves in four risk groups, switched in and out by an ensemble of 20/50/100/200-day EMAs, and levered to a 40% volatility target with hard caps on crypto.

Backtest October 2015 – November 2026, monthly, net of trading costs and borrowing. The target was chosen on 2015–2020 and tested unchanged on 2021–2026. Not investment advice.

SpaceX: the core growth asset

SpaceX (SPCX) since its listing

  • Five months can't be backtested. The space sleeve uses a chain of public proxies, equal-weighting whatever existed on each day:
    • ITA and XAR aerospace ETFs (2006+);
    • UFO and ROKT space ETFs (2019+);
    • ARKX and Rocket Lab (2021+);
    • XOVR and DXYZ, funds that held pre-IPO SpaceX shares (2024+);
    • SPCX itself from June 2026.
  • The sleeve scales between half and full size with how many of its 20/50/100/200-day EMAs it is above. SpaceX's volatility since listing (about 80% a year) is close to Bitcoin's, so size it like crypto, not like an index fund.

How it is run

  • Crypto: hold BTC and ETH in proportion to how many of their 20/50/100/200-day EMAs they are above (4 of 4 = full size, 0 of 4 = all cash). It cut the 2018 and 2022 crypto crashes to single-digit losses for the plan.
  • Hard-asset rotation: each month, hold the top 3 of gold, silver, miners, metals, copper, uranium, energy, oil services, platinum, aerospace and space by strength rating. Each is held only while above at least two of its four EMAs. Aerospace, junior gold miners and copper were held most often.
  • Trend: long or short gold, silver, base metals, oil, commodities, energy, miners and BTC, each sized to 10% volatility.
  • Option premium: each month, sell a gold put spread and spend the credit on gold calls (priced from the gold volatility index), plus the VIX spike fade.
  • Below the EMAs, go to cash, not short. Shorting lost to cash in every asset class (see the EMA section).
  • Each Friday: give the four groups equal risk, then scale the book to a 40% volatility target, capped at 6×.

Optimising the EMA per class overfits. The lengths picked on the early years (10 days for crypto and metals, 200 for miners, energy, space and equities) made the plan look better in 2015–20 and worse afterwards: 23.3% against 28.1% a year. The best lengths simply moved around between periods.
  • Use an ensemble instead. Size each position by how many of its 20, 50, 100 and 200-day EMAs the price is above. It needs no fitting. It beat the single 50-day average for crypto, equities, space and energy, roughly tied elsewhere, and lifted the plan to 28.7% a year in the test period, with a smaller drawdown (−30%) and milder losing years.
  • Below the EMAs, hold cash. Shorting lost to cash in every asset class, with one near-tie (industrial metals at 20 days, half short). Overweighting comes from the volatility target, not from shorting.

Rolling-window optimisation

Every January, the walk-forward version re-picks the weighting method, volatility target (20–40%) and leverage cap (3×, 4× or 6×) from the previous 3 years only. It chooses the highest trailing return with a drawdown no worse than −30%.

Here re-optimising came close to the fixed plan: 26.4% against 28.7% a year in 2021–2026. That's because it kept picking the same 35–40% targets. As with Goal-25, it never beat the fixed setting, so fix the settings.

  • Against QQQ, SPY and gold: large and statistically clear alpha (t-stats of 3.1–3.7). The plan captured more of their up months than their down months.
  • Against Bitcoin: the plan made less (−21% a year) but with under half of BTC's volatility. It caught only 24% of BTC's down months. Owning BTC outright paid more in the boom, but lost 75% in 2018 and 64% in 2022.
  • Diversification is real: crypto, hard-asset trend, the gold option sleeve and the VIX fade are nearly uncorrelated (mostly below 0.3). That's why the unlevered core reached a Sharpe of 1.40 over the full period and 1.00 in 2021–2026.

Risks and how to run it

  • Crypto crash days: a day like 12 March 2020 (BTC −40%, ETH −45%) costs about 18–21% of the account at the capped sizes. Never lift the crypto caps.
  • Metals crash days: the worst day in the backtest was 30 January 2026 (−16%), when gold and silver collapsed together. The gold option sleeve, the rotation and the trend sleeve all lost at once.
  • SpaceX has five months of trading history. It has already fallen 49% from peak at times, and its volatility is about 80% a year. Treat the space sleeve as high-risk growth until it has a longer track record.
  • Practical tools:
    • spot BTC/ETH ETFs (IBIT, ETHA) or CME micro futures for crypto;
    • ETFs for the rotation and space sleeves;
    • micro futures (MGC, SIL, MCL, MES) for trend;
    • GLD listed options for the put-spread sleeve;
    • a capped SVXY position for the spike fade.
  • Modelled option prices, free data and a short history (11 years): paper-trade it first.
