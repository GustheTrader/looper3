Goal-25: higher Sharpe, 25% a year

Eight return streams with risk balanced across four groups, levered to a 40% volatility target with hard limits on each sleeve. Below: the equity curves, what happens when we re-optimise on rolling windows, and the edge we can realistically deliver.

Backtest March 2008 – September 2026, monthly, net of trading costs and borrowing. Option sleeves use modelled prices calibrated to real indices. Not investment advice.

The plan

Sleeves and current sizing (× account value)

Sizes are notional exposure as a multiple of the account, so the total can exceed 1×. Hard limits: spike fade ≤ 0.25×, momentum ≤ 1×, each put-spread sleeve ≤ 0.5×.

How it is run

  • Each Friday, size every sleeve by the inverse of its 63-day volatility, so each of the four groups (growth, option premium, trend, momentum) carries equal risk.
  • Then scale the whole book to a 40% annual volatility target, at most 8× total exposure.
  • Each month end: re-pick the leaders, refresh the trend signals, roll the put spread into calls, and sell the put-write.
  • Daily: check the 50-day averages and the spike-fade signal.

Rolling-window optimisation

Can re-tuning help? Every January, the walk-forward version looks back over the previous 3 or 5 years only. It re-picks the weighting method (four-group, risk parity, or trailing-Sharpe budgets), the volatility target (25–40%) and the leverage cap (6× or 8×), choosing the highest trailing return whose drawdown was no worse than −30%. It then trades that choice for the next year.

Re-optimising hurt. Both walk-forward versions trailed the fixed plan by about 4–5% a year and fell short of QQQ. After the 2022 drawdown, the 3-year version cut its volatility target to 25% for 2024–25 and missed the rebound. The same thing happened in the momentum research: chasing the recent best setting buys noise. Fix the settings, and don't re-tune them yearly. The walk-forward rows only start in 2012 (3-year) and 2014 (5-year), once enough history exists.

What to expect live. Live strategies usually keep only part of their backtest Sharpe, because of fills, model error and luck in the sample. A 25% cut is a common rule of thumb. At this volatility, that points to about 15–22% a year, with 25% in roughly half of 5-year periods, rather than a sure 25%. Drawdowns of 25–35% should be expected along the way.

Risks and how to run it

  • Leverage of about 5× total exposure needs a futures or portfolio-margin account. Practical tools: micro futures (MES, MNQ, ZN, GC, 6E) for trend; LEAPS and put-spread-funded calls for the equity sleeves; SVXY, capped, for the spike fade; cash-secured SPY puts for put-write.
  • Gap risk: a February-2018 style VIX shock costs about 12.5% of the account with the limits in place. Without them it would be 72%. Never lift the spike-fade limit.
  • Put spreads sold to buy calls: a fast fall through the short put loses the full spread width that month.
  • Modelled option prices and free data: results for the option sleeves are estimates. Validate on real quotes in a paper-trading account before committing capital.
  • Momentum long/short uses survivorship-free research portfolios. Live trading needs a broad stock basket or ETF pair, and will differ.
